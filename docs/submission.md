**Long Description**  
Small shop owners struggle with inventory: overstocking ties up cash, while understocking loses sales. Restock Copilot Lite solves this by analyzing historical sales to recommend suggested reorder quantities. We evaluated three transparent forecasting methods: a 4-week baseline average, a seasonal trend adjuster (which added error because we only have two years of data), and a smoothed exponential average (alpha 0.2, "smoothed trend", no seasonal ratio). The smoothed method was chosen on two validation windows before the test window, after the first test results had been seen, reducing validation error from 0.6063 to 0.5796; on the test window the gain was smaller (0.5077 vs 0.5166). The forecast-error margin of smoothed over baseline is small and we do not claim it is significant. We tested the methods via an 8-week replay simulation during the pre-Christmas build-up starting 2011-10-10. Results show: Baseline had forecast error 0.5166, 29,722 lost units (£39,922.05 lost value), and £268.19 weekly leftover value (209.03 units); Seasonal had forecast error 0.6395, 26,933 lost units (£34,438.94 lost value), and £398.40 weekly leftover value (339.33 units); Smoothed had forecast error 0.5077, 32,728 lost units (£46,977.69 lost value), and £218.08 weekly leftover value (180.19 units). The pre-fixed rule was "lowest forecast error", so smoothed won and drives suggested orders, but we do not say it is better for the business. In fact, the most accurate method (smoothed, 0.5077) leaves the most demand unmet (about 23.6% of 138,538 units), baseline about 21.5%, and seasonal about 19.4%. The results are mixed. Note that lost_value is an 8-week total and leftover_value is a weekly snapshot; they cannot be subtracted and no method is a net win. No method is called better except on forecast error, and that is the only sense.
